The first weeks in a new place are busy. There are new streets, a new job or a new school, and a pile of things to sort out. This short guide lists the most useful first steps for people who have just moved to Norwich or anywhere in Norfolk. Each step links to the official page, so you can check the details yourself.

## 1. Register with a GP

A GP is a family doctor. In England you register with a GP surgery near your home, and it is free.

The NHS says you **do not need ID, proof of address or proof of immigration status** to register. You give basic details such as your name, date of birth and address. If you do not have a permanent address, you can use a temporary one.

You can find a surgery and register online on the [NHS website](https://www.nhs.uk/service-search/find-a-gp). It takes about 15 minutes for each person. You can also ask for a paper form at the surgery. For children, the surgery may ask for proof of ID and address, such as a birth certificate.

Do this early. It is much easier to see a doctor when you are already registered.

## 2. Know when to call 111 and 999

- **999** is for life-threatening emergencies, such as a serious accident, a stroke or a heart attack.
- **NHS 111** is for when you need medical help now but it is not a 999 emergency. You can call 111, use [111.nhs.uk](https://111.nhs.uk/) or use the NHS App. They will tell you where to go.

Save both numbers in your phone today.

## 3. Get your National Insurance number

Your National Insurance number is your personal number for tax and benefits. It stays the same for your whole life.

You can apply on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/apply-national-insurance-number) if you live in the UK, have the right to work, and are working, looking for work or have a job offer. You can start work before your number arrives if you can prove your right to work. It can take up to 4 weeks.

If you have an eVisa or an old biometric residence permit, you may already have a number. Check your UKVI account or the back of the old card before you apply.

## 4. Check your eVisa and UKVI account

The UK has replaced biometric residence permit cards with eVisas. If you have a visa or status, you prove it online through a UKVI account. If you have not set one up yet, follow the steps on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/get-access-evisa). Keep your login details safe.

## 5. Council tax: who sends the bill

Most homes pay council tax. In Norfolk, the bill comes from your **district or city council**, not from Norfolk County Council. There are seven:

- Norwich City Council
- Broadland
- South Norfolk
- Breckland
- Great Yarmouth
- King's Lynn and West Norfolk
- North Norfolk

If you live in Norwich, you pay [Norwich City Council](https://www.norwich.gov.uk/council-tax).

**If you live alone, you can get 25% off your bill.** This is the single person discount. It is not automatic, so you need to apply to your council. Other discounts may also apply, so read your bill carefully.

## 6. School places

Norfolk County Council handles school admissions for the whole county. If you arrive in the middle of the school year, look for **in-year admissions** on the [council's admissions page](https://www.norfolk.gov.uk/admissions). The same page lists the deadlines for the main application rounds.

## 7. Universal Credit

Universal Credit is a monthly payment to help with living costs. You apply online on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/apply-universal-credit). Whether you can claim depends on several things, **including your immigration status**, so read the [eligibility page](https://www.gov.uk/universal-credit/eligibility) first. Free help to make a claim is available through Citizens Advice.

## 8. Free help in Norwich

Norwich has good free services. These are a few that are useful for newcomers:

- **[Norfolk Citizens Advice](https://www.ncab.org.uk/)** gives free, confidential advice on benefits, debt, housing, work and more. Their advice line is 0800 144 8848. Opening times vary, so call first.
- **[New Routes Integration](https://newroutes.org.uk/)** supports refugees, asylum seekers and migrants in Norwich with English classes, advice sessions and wellbeing groups.
- **[Norfolk Community Law Service](https://www.ncls.co.uk/)** is a Norwich charity that offers free legal advice in some areas of law. The areas it can help with change, so check its website.
- **[The Millennium Library](https://www.norfolk.gov.uk/article/40217/Norfolk-and-Norwich-Millennium-Library)** at The Forum has free computers and wifi for library members. You can [join online](https://www.norfolk.gov.uk/computers).

## 9. Immigration questions

Be careful who you ask about visas and status. In the UK it is a criminal offence to give immigration advice unless you are qualified. Only advisers regulated by the **Immigration Advice Authority** (called the OISC until January 2025), and solicitors and barristers, can do it.

You can check if an adviser is regulated on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/find-an-immigration-adviser). If someone offers immigration help and is not on the register, do not pay them.
